## Low Water

At the bottom of the spring tide the marsh shows its floor. Channels that are
rivers at noon become a set of shallow grooves, and you can walk out along them
into the middle of what was, four hours ago, a bay.

It feels like trespass. The light is wrong, the sound is wrong — no lapping, only
the tick and suck of draining mud — and every few yards something moves away from
your boot that you never see whole.

I do not stay long. The turn is quick here, and the water comes back not as a wave
but as a rising everywhere at once, which is far harder to notice and much harder
to outwalk.
