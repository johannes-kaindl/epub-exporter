## The Tide Line

The marsh keeps no fixed border. Twice a day the sea walks up into the grass and
twice a day it withdraws, and the line it leaves behind is never quite the line it
left the morning before. I have tried to mark it with stakes. The stakes are
honest enough; it is the water that will not agree to anything.

What the tide leaves is a thin braid of stems, feathers, and small white shells,
laid along the slope as neatly as if a hand had done it. You can follow it for a
mile. It tells you, better than any instrument, how high the water rose in the
night and how quietly it must have gone.

I came here to write about birds and have written mostly about water. That seems
to be the way of the place. The birds are the visitors. The water lives here.
